Vocabulary I — Matching

Matching — Tips & Strategy

20 words → match to definitions a–t

  • Read ALL definitions before choosing — get the full picture first
  • Match the easiest ones first — eliminate them as you go
  • Look for clue words in definitions: "unable to" → indecisive · "not influenced" → detached
  • Watch for negatives — "in-" prefix often means NOT (indecisive = not decisive)
  • Stuck? Use elimination — cross out matched letters to narrow it down

Vocabulary I — Matching

Tricky Pairs — Don't Confuse These!

  • Rational vs. Pragmatic → Rational = pure reason/logic · Pragmatic = practical sense
  • Thoughtful vs. Methodical → Thoughtful = reflects carefully · Methodical = follows a process/system
  • Instinctive vs. Impulsive → Instinctive = natural gut feeling · Impulsive = reckless, no thought at all
  • Self-contained vs. Detached → Self-contained = rely on self · Detached = not emotionally involved with others
  • Flexible vs. Conventional → complete opposites: flexible adapts to change · conventional follows the norm

Vocabulary III — Word Families

Word Families — Patterns to Know

Learn the suffix → you can guess the form

  • -or / -er / -ant / -ist = PERSON NOUN (who does it) → Communicator · Analyst · Consultant · Economist
  • -tion / -ation / -ment = NOUN (the action/result) → Communication · Innovation · Development
  • -ive / -al / -able / -ary = ADJECTIVE → Communicative · Analytical · Transferable · Voluntary
  • -ize / -ise / -ate = VERB → Economize · Innovate · Participate
  • Tip: if the answer slot needs an adjective, look for -ive/-al/-able/-ary endings

Grammar — Future Tenses

Will vs. Be Going To

  • WILL — on-the-spot: "Oh no, I forgot! I'll buy it now." (decide right now)
  • WILL — prediction: "I think she will win." (no evidence, just opinion)
  • WILL — promise/request: "I won't get mad again. I promise." / "Will you close the window?"
  • BE GOING TO — pre-planned: "We're going to meet at 7pm." (already decided before)
  • BE GOING TO — evidence: "Look at those clouds — it's going to rain." (you can see it)
  • Key rule: WILL = decided NOW · BE GOING TO = decided BEFORE
Grammar — Present Perfect

Present Perfect — Simple vs. Continuous

  • Simple (have/has + V3) — focus on RESULT: States · completed actions with quantities · "I've finished." "She has written 3 reports."
  • Continuous (have/has been + V-ing) — focus on ACTION: How long? · still ongoing · temporary · recent evidence · "He has been working all week."
  • Both use: for/since · recently/all day/this week · unfinished time periods
  • Continuous only: "How long have you been…?" · temporary situations
  • Simple = RESULT matters → "I've painted the house" (done) · Continuous = ACTION matters → "I've been painting" (hands dirty)

Grammar — Passive Voice

Passive Voice — Tricky Points

  • How to form: Move object → new subject · add correct "be" · add V3 · "by + agent" only if agent matters
  • Present Perfect Passive: has/have + been + V3 → "Graduates have been recruited."
  • Modal Passive: modal + be + V3 → "Interviews can be conducted." / "This should be done."
  • Common mistake: ❌ "was been done" → ✅ "was done" · ❌ "is been made" → ✅ "is being made"
  • Omit "by someone / by people" when the agent is not important or unknown
Grammar — Conditionals

First Conditional — Real & Possible

If + Present Simple, Will + base verb

  • Use for: Real and possible future situations
  • Examples: "If you stop asking, I will explain." · "If there is an emergency, I will call you."
  • Negative: "If we don't meet, we won't finish our work." · "If it isn't raining, delivery will arrive."
  • Critical rule: NEVER use "will" in the IF clause → ❌ "If you will stop…" → ✅ "If you stop…"
  • Memory: IF = condition (present simple) · RESULT = will + verb
Grammar — Conditionals

Second Conditional — Hypothetical / Unreal

If + Past Simple, Would + base verb

  • Use for: Imaginary or unlikely situations — not expected to happen
  • Examples: "If I were a superstar, people would know me." · "If I had superpower, I would fly to Paris."
  • To be: Always use "were" for ALL subjects in exams → ✅ "If I were you…" / "If she were here…"
  • Negative: "If Lin weren't my friend, I wouldn't trust her."
  • 1st = real possibility → "If it rains…" · 2nd = not real/hypothetical → "If I were a bird…" (I'm not)
Grammar — Modal Verbs

Modals — Present Forms

ModalUseKey Point
MustStrong obligation — speaker gives the ruleYOU decide and tell someone → "You must be quiet."
Mustn'tProhibition — NOT allowed at allForbidden → "You mustn't arrive late."
ShouldAdvice — recommendedGentle suggestion → "You should take an umbrella."
Have to / Need toExternal obligation — rules, duties, regulationsRule from outside (law/company) → "I have to register."

Modals — Past Forms

PresentPast FormExample
