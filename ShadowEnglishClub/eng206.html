Fill in the Gap Tips

  • Read the full sentence before picking a word
  • Identify: is it a noun, verb, or adjective?
  • Eliminate wrong answers first
  • Check: does it make logical sense in context?

Reading Skills

Reading Skills

Skimming:

  • Read FAST to get the main idea
  • Don't read every word
  • Focus on: title, first + last sentence of each paragraph
  • Goal: understand the general topic in under 2 minutes

Reading Skills

Scanning:

  • Search for SPECIFIC info (names, numbers, dates, keywords)
  • Eyes move fast — don't read everything
  • Goal: find one piece of info quickly
  • Tip: know what you're looking for BEFORE you scan

Reading Skills

Making Inferences:

  • Reading BETWEEN the lines — answer is NOT directly stated
  • Combine: what the text says + logic
  • Ask: "What does this suggest?"
  • Class example: teacher says test is from the textbook → inference: students didn't study

Reading Skills

Paraphrasing:

  • Rewrite in YOUR OWN WORDS — same meaning, different structure
  • Don't just swap a few words — rewrite the whole sentence
  • Check: does it still mean the same thing?

Reading Skills

Summarizing:

  • Main points ONLY — cut out details and examples
  • Usually 3–5 sentences for one passage/text
  • Use your own words
  • Ask: "What is the author's main message?"

Reading Skills

Linguistic Hedging:

  • Cautious language to avoid absolute statements
  • Words: might, seem, possibly, tend to, can, could, may
  • Common in academic writing — shows uncertainty
  • Example: "This may suggest..." / "It tends to..."

Reading Skills — Bonus

When you come across unknown/unfamiliar words in the reading text?

  • Use context clues (surrounding sentences)
  • Look at word parts (prefix / suffix / root)
  • Skip it and come back — another part may help
  • Make an educated guess from context

Reading Comprehension

TEXT 1: "Are Computers Alive?" — Main Idea

  • Core debate: can computers THINK?
  • Some say computers = "high-speed idiots" — fast but need specific instructions
  • AI researchers question: what IS thinking?
  • Animals show basic thinking via brain activity tests
  • Conclusion: computers are smarter than most life forms in certain tasks — but not all

Reading Comprehension

TEXT 1: "Are Computers Alive?" — How to Read It

  • Technique 1 — Track the ARGUMENT structure:
    • Look for: however, but, on the other hand → signals a shift in opinion
    • There are TWO sides: computers CAN think vs. only humans can think
    • Don't mix up who argues what
  • Technique 2 — Track who says WHAT:
    • Different researchers have different views
    • MCQ and True/False often test if you confuse one researcher's claim with another's
    • Tip: mentally label each paragraph: "this side says X, that side says Y"

Reading Comprehension

TEXT 1: "Are Computers Alive?" — Answering Tips

  • MCQ:
    • Words like always/never/all → usually WRONG
    • Words like some/may/tend to → often correct for this type of debate text
  • True/False:
    • Find the EXACT line in the text — don't answer from memory or general knowledge
    • Partially true statements are a common trick — read the full sentence
  • Critical Thinking:
    • Give opinion + evidence: "In my opinion… because the text states…"
    • Acknowledge the other side before concluding

Reading Comprehension

TEXT 2: "To Paint Is to Live" — Main Idea

  • A stone sculptor well known in San Francisco. Personality revealed through behavior, not directly stated.
  • Key traits (inferred from text):
    • Well known in SF → Para D: built major city landmarks
    • Sense of humor → Para G: promised a dragon fireplace on the spot; "I told him it takes two"
    • Emotional → Para E: "every time I talk about it, I get excited… it was a labor of love"
    • Perfectionist → "every line had to match" — if one stone broke, restart everything
    • Risk taker → relocated entire operation to Mexico for a bigger space
    • Client-focused → drove the sculpture back to the US himself

Reading Comprehension

TEXT 2: "To Paint Is to Live" — How to Read It

  • Technique 1 — Inference by paragraph:
    • Don't just say "he's a perfectionist" — point to Para + line as evidence
    • The questions will ask: "Which paragraph supports this inference?" — know where each trait is
  • Technique 2 — Comprehension questions:
    • Scan for KEYWORDS from the question
    • Go to that paragraph, read it carefully
    • Answer using words close to the original — don't add outside info

Reading Comprehension

TEXT 2: "To Paint Is to Live" — Answering Tips

  • Comprehension:
    • Always know WHERE your answer is (Para X, line Y)
    • Copy key phrases from the text — don't paraphrase unless asked
  • Critical Thinking example:
    • Q: "Is the sculptor a perfectionist? Why?"
    • A: Yes — Para X states "every line had to match" and if any stone broke, he would restart the entire piece from scratch
  • True/False trick:
    • Some statements are PARTIALLY true — read the full claim carefully
    • Ex: "The sculptor built landmarks in New York" → FALSE (it's San Francisco, Para D)
