Fill in the Gap Tips

  • Read the full sentence before picking a word
  • Identify: is it a noun, verb, or adjective?
  • Eliminate wrong answers first
  • Check: does it make logical sense in context?

Reading Skills

Reading Skills

Reading Skills — 5

  • Skimming: Read FAST to get the main idea
  • Don't read every word
  • Focus on: title, first + last sentence of each paragraph
  • Goal: understand the general topic in under 2 minutes
Reading Skills

Reading Skills — 5

  • Scanning: Search for SPECIFIC info (names, numbers, dates, keywords)
  • Eyes move fast — don't read everything
  • Goal: find one piece of info quickly
  • Tip: know what you're looking for BEFORE you scan
Reading Skills

Reading Skills — 5

  • Making Inferences: Reading BETWEEN the lines — answer is NOT directly stated
  • Combine: what the text says + logic
  • Ask: "What does this suggest?"
  • Class example: teacher says test is from the textbook → inference: students didn't study
Reading Skills

Reading Skills — 5

  • Paraphrasing: Rewrite in YOUR OWN WORDS — same meaning, different structure
  • Don't just swap a few words — rewrite the whole sentence
  • Check: does it still mean the same thing?
Reading Skills

Reading Skills — 5

  • Summarizing: Main points ONLY — cut out details and examples
  • Usually 3–5 sentences for one passage
  • Use your own words
  • Ask: "What is the author's main message?"
Reading Skills

Reading Skills — 5

  • Linguistic Hedging: Cautious language to avoid absolute statements
  • Words: might, seem, possibly, tend to, can, could, may
  • Common in academic writing — shows uncertainty or possibility
  • Example: "This may suggest..." / "It tends to..."
Reading Skills

Reading Skills — Bonus

When you come across unknown/unfamiliar words in the reading text?

  • Use context clues (surrounding sentences)
  • Look at word parts (prefix / suffix / root)
  • Skip it and come back — another part may help
  • Make an educated guess from context

Reading Comprehension

TEXT 1 — Are Computers Alive?

Main Idea

  • Core debate: can computers THINK?
  • Some say computers = "high-speed idiots" — fast but need specific instructions
  • AI researchers question: what IS thinking?
  • Animals show basic thinking via brain activity tests
  • Conclusion: computers are smarter than most life forms in certain tasks — but not all
Reading Comprehension

TEXT 1 — How to Read It

  • Technique 1 — Track the ARGUMENT structure: Look for however, but, on the other hand → signals a shift in opinion
  • There are TWO sides: computers CAN think vs. only humans can think — don't mix them up
  • Technique 2 — Track who says WHAT: Different researchers have different views
  • MCQ and True/False often test if you confuse one researcher's claim with another's
  • Tip: mentally label each paragraph — "this side says X, that side says Y"
Reading Comprehension

TEXT 1 — Answering Tips

  • MCQ: Words like always/never/all → usually WRONG. Words like some/may/tend to → often correct
  • True/False: Find the EXACT line in the text — don't answer from memory or general knowledge
  • Partially true statements are a common trick — read the full sentence carefully
  • Critical Thinking: Give opinion + evidence: "In my opinion… because the text states…"
  • Acknowledge the other side before concluding
Reading Comprehension

TEXT 2 — To Paint Is to Live

Key traits inferred from the text

  • Well known in SF → Para D: built major city landmarks
  • Sense of humor → Para G: promised a dragon fireplace; "I told him it takes two"
  • Emotional → Para E: "every time I talk about it, I get excited… it was a labor of love"
  • Perfectionist → "every line had to match" — if one stone broke, restart everything
  • Risk taker → relocated entire operation to Mexico for a bigger space
Reading Comprehension

TEXT 2 — How to Read It

  • Technique 1 — Inference by paragraph: Don't just say "he's a perfectionist" — point to Para + line as evidence
  • Questions will ask: "Which paragraph supports this inference?" — know where each trait is
  • Technique 2 — Comprehension questions: Scan for KEYWORDS from the question
  • Go to that paragraph, read carefully — answer using words close to the original
Reading Comprehension

TEXT 2 — Answering Tips

  • Comprehension: Always know WHERE your answer is (Para X, line Y)
  • Copy key phrases from the text — don't paraphrase unless asked
  • Critical Thinking example: Q: "Is the sculptor a perfectionist? Why?"
  • A: Yes — "every line had to match" and if any stone broke, he would restart the entire piece from scratch
  • True/False trick: Some statements are PARTIALLY true — Ex: "built landmarks in New York" → FALSE (it's San Francisco, Para D)
