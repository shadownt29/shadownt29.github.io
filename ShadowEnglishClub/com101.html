Part I — MCQ

Mindfulness & Nonjudgmental Awareness

Being fully present with openness — no judgment

  • Mindfulness = intentionally present in the moment with openness and non-judgment
  • Nonjudgmental awareness = observing thoughts and feelings without labeling them good/bad
  • 5 Facets (Baer): Observing · Describing · Acting with awareness · Non-judging · Nonreactivity
  • 3 Components (Shapiro): Attention (present focus) · Intention (knowing why) · Attitude (COAL: Curiosity, Openness, Acceptance, Love)
  • Exam: manager notices frustration but doesn't judge = nonjudgmental awareness in action
Part I — MCQ

Cognitive Dishonesty

Being dishonest with yourself about your own thoughts or feelings

  • Telling yourself "I'm fine" when you're not
  • Justifying a bad decision instead of admitting it
  • Pretending to listen while distracted
  • Result: Incongruent communication — what you say ≠ what you feel
  • Opposite = cognitive honesty: acknowledging your real feelings and biases before communicating
Part I — MCQ

Passive Accommodation

Going along with others' wishes while suppressing your own needs

  • Avoid disagreement at all costs
  • Say "yes" even when you mean "no"
  • Prioritize keeping the peace over honesty
  • Different from assertiveness: Assertive = express your needs clearly · Passive = suppress them entirely
  • Short-term peace → long-term resentment
Part I — MCQ

Hearing vs. Listening

HURIER Model — 6 stages of listening

  • Hearing = passive, automatic, physiological — sound enters your ears
  • Listening = active, intentional, cognitive — you process and give meaning
  • HURIER: Hearing → Understanding → Remembering → Interpreting → Evaluating → Responding
  • Biggest hurdle = Evaluating stage: personal biases block true listening
  • You can HEAR without LISTENING — hearing is step 1, not the whole process
Part I — MCQ

Congruence · Incongruence · High/Low Context

  • Congruence = verbal and nonverbal messages match → authentic communication
  • Incongruence = mismatch (e.g., saying "I'm fine" while looking tense)
  • High-Context Culture = implicit meaning, nonverbal cues matter, politeness is key (Cambodia, Japan)
  • Low-Context Culture = explicit verbal meaning, directness valued (USA, Germany)
  • Assertive = expressing needs clearly and respectfully — the ideal communication style
Part I — MCQ

Conflict Management Styles

ABCs Model — Avoiders · Battlers · Collaborators

  • Avoiders = evade conflict entirely · tactics: denial, topic shifting, ambivalence, stalling
  • Battlers (Distributive) = win-lose · tactics: faulting, threats, sarcasm, blame
  • Collaborators (Integrative) = win-win · tactics: empathy, problem-solving, accepting responsibility
  • Thomas-Kilmann map: Competing=Battler · Avoiding=Avoider · Collaborating/Compromising/Accommodating=Collaborator
  • STLC Model: Stop → Think → Listen → Communicate
Part I — MCQ

Social Penetration Theory

Altman & Taylor (1973) — relationships deepen through self-disclosure like peeling an onion

  • Breadth = range of topics discussed · Depth = how personal those topics go
  • Stages: Orientation → Exploratory Affective → Affective → Stable → Depenetration
  • More breadth + depth = closer relationship
  • 3 factors affect disclosure: personality · reward/cost · situational context
  • Onion analogy: outer layer = visible traits, inner core = deepest desires and fears
Part I — MCQ

Computer-Mediated Communication (CMC)

Communication that happens through technology

  • Synchronous = real-time (video call, live chat) · Asynchronous = delayed (email, text)
  • CMC filters out nonverbal cues → we fill gaps with our own assumptions → often wrong
  • Pros: Accessible · flexible · connects across distance · time to think before replying
  • Cons: Loss of nonverbal cues · easier to misread tone · less emotional depth · more conflict
  • Emoticons were the first attempt to recover nonverbal meaning in text-based CMC
Part I — MCQ

FIRO Theory

Fundamental Interpersonal Relations Orientations — William Schutz

  • Control = need to influence others and situations (not manipulation — it's healthy influence)
  • Inclusion = need to belong to groups (family, friends, workplace, communities)
  • Affection = need to give and receive emotional warmth and positive regard
  • Every interpersonal relationship serves at least one of these 3 needs
  • Memory trick: CIA — Control, Inclusion, Affection
Part I — MCQ

Communication Styles

Passive · Aggressive · Assertive · Passive-Aggressive · Avoidant

  • Passive = suppress needs, say "yes" when meaning "no" → resentment
  • Aggressive = express needs in hostile, dominating ways → damaged relationships
  • Assertive = clear, respectful, direct → mutual respect (IDEAL)
  • Passive-Aggressive = appear compliant, act out resentment indirectly
  • Avoidant = withdraw from communication entirely to escape discomfort

Cognitive Complexity & Cognitive Bias

  • Cognitive Complexity = ability to understand a situation from multiple perspectives; to express the same idea in different ways; mentally flexible
  • Cognitive Bias = systematic errors in thinking that distort judgment and decision-making; mental shortcuts that skew perception
  • Key difference: Cognitive complexity = a STRENGTH · Cognitive bias = a THINKING ERROR
Part II — Vocab

Types of Bias

  • Information Bias = seeking or interpreting information that confirms your existing beliefs; ignoring contradictory evidence
  • Cognitive Bias = broader category — any consistent pattern of deviation from rational judgment
  • Example: assuming someone is late because they're irresponsible (ignoring external causes like traffic or emergencies)
Part II — Vocab

Empathic Listening

Also called Therapeutic Listening — being a sounding board

  • Fully focus on the other person's emotions and perspective
  • Don't jump to solutions — acknowledge feelings first
  • Reflect back: "It sounds like you're frustrated because…"
  • Empathic ≠ Sympathetic · Empathic = feel WITH them · Sympathetic = feel FOR them

Kinesics

Scientific study of nonverbal communication through body movements

  • Includes: gestures · facial expressions · posture · eye contact (oculesics)
  • Ekman's 6 universal expressions: happiness · sadness · anger · fear · disgust · surprise
  • Open body posture = approachable · Closed = defensive
  • Exam example: a coach gesturing in stands (Serena Williams case) = kinesics; judge made attribution error by misreading it
Part II — Vocab

Fundamental Attribution Error

Explaining others' behavior through internal character, ignoring external factors

  • Friend is late → "They're irresponsible" (ignoring: traffic, emergency, wrong time)
  • You are late → "There was traffic" (you blame the external situation)
  • Pattern: Others' bad behavior = their fault (internal) · Your bad behavior = the situation's fault (external)
  • One of the most common cognitive errors in communication — affects how we judge others unfairly
Part II — Vocab

Emotional Intelligence (EQ)

Salovey & Mayer (1990) — popularized by Daniel Goleman

  • Definition: Ability to recognize, understand, manage, and use emotions — yours and others'
  • 4 components: Perceiving emotions · Using emotions · Understanding emotions · Managing emotions
  • Higher EQ → better relationships, conflict management, leadership
  • Higher EQ = more sociable, less socially anxious, higher-quality relationships
Part II — Vocab

Self-Fulfilling Prophecy

When your expectations make themselves come true

  • You form an expectation ("This professor is mean")
  • You behave based on it (sit in back, don't participate)
  • The outcome reflects your expectation (no connection with professor)
  • You confirm: "See? They ARE mean" — even though YOU caused it
  • Formula: expectation → behavior → outcome → confirms expectation
Part II — Vocab

Relational Dialectics

Baxter & Montgomery (1996) — ongoing tensions between opposing needs in relationships

  • Integration vs. Separation — closeness vs. independence
  • Predictability vs. Novelty — routine vs. excitement
  • Openness vs. Closedness — sharing vs. privacy
  • Similarity vs. Difference — common ground vs. growth
  • These tensions never fully resolve — they must be MANAGED, not solved (both/and approach)
Part II — Vocab

Communication Competence

The ability to communicate both effectively AND appropriately

  • Effectiveness = achieving your communication goal
  • Appropriateness = doing it in a way that fits the situation and relationship
  • Traits of competent communicators: cognitive complexity · self-monitoring · empathy · adaptability
  • You can be effective but inappropriate — that is NOT competent
Part II — Vocab

Mindfulness

Intentionally present in the moment with openness, curiosity, and non-judgment

  • 3 Components (Shapiro): Attention (present focus) · Intention (knowing WHY) · Attitude (COAL)
  • COAL: Curiosity · Openness · Acceptance · Love
  • Mindful Communication = planful, present, non-judging, able to adapt in the moment
  • Mindfulness ≠ just meditation — it's a state of mind in ANY interaction

Part III — Unscramble

5 Cognitive Biases

The tendency of something… — unscramble the letters

  • Confirmation Bias — seeking info that confirms what you already believe, ignoring contradictions
  • Anchoring Bias — over-relying on the FIRST piece of info received when deciding
  • Availability Heuristic — judging likelihood based on how easily an example comes to mind
  • Negativity Bias / Self-Serving Bias — bad events hit harder · taking credit for wins, blaming outside for losses
  • Optimism Bias / Bandwagon Effect — thinking bad things happen to others · adopting beliefs because "everyone does it"

Reading — Mindfulness in Modern Relationships

5 True/False + 5 MCQ — key ideas the passage will cover

  • Mindfulness reduces reactivity → better conflict management in relationships
  • Being present = not distracted by phones, past, or future thoughts
  • Nonjudgmental listening improves relational satisfaction

  • MCQ strategy: Eliminate absolute answers first · choose the answer closest to what the passage actually says
